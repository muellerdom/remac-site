What ReMac does

ReMac turns a Mac that is connected to a TV or a large display into something you use from the sofa. On the Mac, TV mode shows a home screen with large tiles; on the iPhone, ReMac Remote is the remote. You need both apps.

  • Tiles for streaming websites and Mac apps, opened full screen.
  • Touchpad and keyboard on the iPhone, for the pointer, scrolling and typing.
  • Volume, speakers and display: change the volume, pick an AirPlay speaker or another screen.
  • Screensavers that keep a still picture off the screen. See them all.
  • Sleep timer, scenes and VPN switching, also through Siri and Control Center.

The Mac app is free. The iPhone app is free too; a one-time purchase, ReMac Pro, will unlock extras such as VPN switching, scenes and Siri.

Getting started

  1. Download ReMac on the Mac you want to use as a TV, drag it to Applications and open it. A small TV icon appears in the menu bar; in TV mode ReMac fills the whole screen.
  2. In the “Welcome to ReMac” window, click “Allow…” and switch ReMac on in the Accessibility list that opens. The remote needs this to move the pointer and type.
  3. Install ReMac Remote on your iPhone from the App Store. Allow Local Network and Camera when the iPhone asks.
  4. On the Mac, choose “Pair iPhone…” in the ReMac menu (the TV icon in the menu bar) or in the welcome window, and scan the code with the iPhone. Check that both show the same six-digit code, then click “Allow” on the Mac.
  5. Press Home on the iPhone: TV mode starts and shows your tiles. The first time you open a streaming tile, sign in to that service on the Mac.

New versions of the Mac app come from this page; it doesn't update itself.

Common questions

The iPhone doesn't find my Mac

Check four things: ReMac is running on the Mac, the Mac is awake, both are on the same Wi-Fi, and ReMac Remote is switched on under Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network on the iPhone. So the Mac stays reachable while its display is off, open ReMac Settings › General › Waking from iPhone and click “Set Up Waking…”. “Wake for network access” alone wakes a sleeping Mac only if an Apple TV or HomePod is on the same network.

The screen stays dark

Touch the touchpad or press Home: the first press only wakes the screen and ends the screensaver. Volume and mute don't wake it. Opening an app wakes the screen and then opens the app; Power wakes it and shows the home screen (with the Power button set to “Leave TV Mode”, the default). This works while the Mac itself is awake. If the Mac shows its login screen, unlock it at the Mac: ReMac never types or stores your password.

Which browsers work for web tiles?

Brave, Vivaldi, Opera, Edge, Chrome, Firefox and Safari. Brave, Vivaldi and Opera work best: videos switch to full screen by themselves. In Edge, Chrome, Firefox and Safari you start full screen yourself. Except in Safari, tiles open in a separate ReMac profile, apart from your own browser windows, so sign in to each streaming service once there. Safari tiles use your normal Safari and need “Allow Control of Safari…” (welcome window or ReMac Settings › Tiles).

Switching VPN locations

ReMac switches between VPN connections you have already set up on the Mac; it doesn't include a VPN. In ReMac Settings › VPN, “Set Up VPN…” walks you through it. Check the terms of your streaming services.
