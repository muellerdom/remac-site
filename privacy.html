Privacy Policy

ReMac Remote (iPhone) and ReMac (Mac). Effective: 18 November 2026.

Summary

ReMac does not collect, sell or share personal data. There are no accounts, no analytics of our own, no advertising and no tracking. The iPhone app talks only to your own Mac, through your network or a direct Wi-Fi connection nearby. In normal use, nothing is sent to the developer. If you have agreed to share analytics with app developers on your iPhone, Apple passes on anonymous crash and usage statistics.

What stays on your iPhone

  • A key that identifies your iPhone to your Mac. It is created on the iPhone; its private part is kept in the iPhone's secure chip (Secure Enclave) and keychain and never leaves the device.
  • The list of paired Macs, your settings, scenes, and the names of your Mac's apps and VPNs, so Siri and Shortcuts can offer them.

Deleting the app removes your settings, scenes and saved names. The pairing keys are in the iPhone's keychain, which iOS can keep after an app is deleted. To end a pairing for good, choose Paired Devices › Revoke… in the ReMac menu on the Mac.

Camera

The camera is used only to scan the pairing code shown on your Mac. Images are processed on the iPhone and are neither stored nor sent anywhere.

Local network

The iPhone app finds your Mac on the local network, or nearby over direct Wi-Fi, and connects to it over an encrypted connection (TLS 1.3) that only your paired Mac can answer. Touchpad input, button presses and text you type are sent to your Mac only.

Siri, Shortcuts and Control Center

iOS makes the names of your tiles, VPNs and scenes available to Siri so you can say them. If you use ReMac with Siri, Apple processes your requests under Apple's privacy policy; ReMac receives only the resulting command, such as “pause”. Shortcuts and Control Center run on the iPhone.

Purchases

ReMac Pro is bought through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment; the developer sees no name or payment details, only anonymous sales figures.

The Mac app

The Mac app keeps its data on that Mac only: its own key, the paired iPhones (name, public key, pairing date, last seen), your tiles and VPN entries, and the separate browser profiles for tiles with their logins and cookies. While you type into a search field in a TV mode tile, ReMac reads the website's list of search suggestions and sends it to your iPhone, so you can pick one there. It never reads the text in the field or anything in other apps, and it keeps none of it. While it runs, the Mac announces itself under its computer name on your network and, unless you turn off “Allow direct Wi-Fi connections”, to devices nearby. The only internet request ReMac makes itself: while a VPN you switched on from the iPhone is connected, it asks an Apple server every 15 seconds whether the internet is reachable. Websites you open in tiles and VPN services you use have their own privacy policies.

ReMac uses these macOS permissions, which you can withdraw in System Settings › Privacy & Security: Accessibility (pointer and keyboard), Automation (Safari and Tunnelblick) and Local Network. To remove everything, choose Paired Devices › Reset Server Identity… in the ReMac menu, then delete ReMac and the folder ~/Library/Application Support/ReMac.

This website

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA). When you open it, GitHub processes your IP address and browser details in its server logs, to deliver the page and keep it secure (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The website sets no cookies and uses no analytics. If you write to us, your message is used only to answer you. Issues on GitHub are public.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correction, deletion and restriction of your personal data, to object to its processing, and to complain to a data protection authority.

Children

ReMac collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes and contact

Changes to this policy are published on this page with a new date. Questions: open an issue on GitHub.
